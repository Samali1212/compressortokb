The Privacy Risks of Online Image Converters (And Why Browser-Based is Safer)

Most "free" online image compressors work the same way: you upload your photo to their server, their server processes it, and you download the result. That single extra step — the upload — is where most of the privacy risk lives.

What Happens When You Upload a Photo

Once an image leaves your device and reaches a remote server, you are trusting that company's infrastructure, staff, and security practices with your file. Depending on the service, that image may be temporarily cached, logged in server access logs, scanned by automated systems, or in some cases retained far longer than their privacy policy implies. For sensitive documents, ID photos, or personal pictures, that is a real exposure most people never think about.

EXIF Data: The Hidden Risk

Photos taken on phones often carry EXIF metadata — GPS coordinates, device model, and timestamp — embedded invisibly in the file. Server-based converters may process and even preserve this metadata without telling you, meaning your exact location could travel along with a photo you thought you were just resizing.

Why Browser-Based Compression Is Different

Compress To KB never uploads your image anywhere. The entire compression pipeline — reading the file, drawing it to a canvas, adjusting quality, and re-encoding it — runs using JavaScript and the HTML5 Canvas API directly inside your own browser tab. Your photo never touches a network request, never reaches our servers (we don't even have an image-processing backend), and disappears from memory the moment you close the page.

What This Means in Practice

  • You can compress sensitive documents (IDs, certificates, contracts) without a third party ever seeing them.
  • The tool works even with no internet connection, once the page has loaded.
  • There is nothing for a data breach to expose, because nothing is ever stored on a server in the first place.

If privacy matters for the images you work with — and it should — a client-side tool is not just a nice-to-have, it is a structurally safer way to compress images. Read our full Privacy Policy for more detail on exactly how the tool handles your data (or rather, doesn't).
